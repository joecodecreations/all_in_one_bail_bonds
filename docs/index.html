How it works

Three steps from your call to release

1

Call us with the basics

Tell us the person's full name, date of birth if you know it, and which county jail they are in. We find the bond amount and the conditions the magistrate or judge set.

2

We explain the cost and the paperwork

You hear the premium and any collateral up front, before you sign anything. The person who signs with us (the cosigner) agrees to make sure the defendant goes to every court date.

3

We post the bond at the jail

A licensed agent posts the bond with the jail. The jail then processes the release. How long that takes is up to the jail, not the bondsman.

What a bail bond costs in North Carolina

State law caps the bail bond premium at 15% of the bond amount. A bondsman may ask for collateral on larger bonds, but may not add application or processing fees on top of the premium.

We accept some payment arrangements. If part of the premium is paid after release, the schedule goes in a written agreement that both sides sign, and you get a copy.

Questions

What families ask us first

How much does a bail bond cost in North Carolina?

North Carolina law caps the bail bond premium at 15% of the bond amount (N.C. Gen. Stat. § 58-71-95). On a $5,000 bond the premium can be no more than $750. A bondsman may also ask for collateral, but may not charge extra application or processing fees. Call us for the exact cost of a specific bond.

Are you really open 24 hours?

Yes. Arrests happen at night, on weekends and on holidays, so a licensed bail agent answers (919) 247-9397 around the clock.

Do I get the premium back when the case is over?

No. The premium is the fee for the bondsman's service and for taking on the risk of the full bond, so it is not returned when the case ends. Collateral is different: under NC law it must be returned within 15 days after the bondsman's liability on the bond finally ends.

Can I make payments?

We accept some payment arrangements. Whether a payment arrangement is available depends on the bond and your situation. When part of the premium is paid after release, North Carolina law requires a written agreement, signed by both sides, that lists every payment date and amount, and you get a copy.

How long does it take to get someone out of jail?

It depends on the jail. We can usually start the paperwork as soon as a bond has been set. After we post the bond, the jail processes the release, and that part is outside any bondsman's control. Busy nights and shift changes can add time.

What does the person who signs the bond agree to?

The cosigner (also called the indemnitor) promises that the defendant will go to every court date. If the defendant misses court and the bond is forfeited, the cosigner can be responsible for the full bond amount.
