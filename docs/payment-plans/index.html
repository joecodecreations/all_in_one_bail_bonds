Bail bond payment arrangements

We accept some payment arrangements. Here is how they work in North Carolina and what the law requires, so there are no surprises.

How a payment arrangement works

If you cannot pay the whole premium at once, ask us about a payment arrangement when you call. If one is available for your bond, part of the premium is paid up front and the rest on a schedule you agree to.

It has to be in writing

North Carolina law protects you here. When any part of the premium is paid after the defendant is released, the bondsman must put the arrangement in a written memorandum that lists the amount still owed and each payment date and amount. Both sides sign and date it, and you get a copy.

What we need from you

  • A cosigner with a steady address and a way to reach them
  • Honest information about the charge and any earlier missed court dates
  • Payments on the dates in the agreement, and a call if something changes

Ask about payment options

Payment questions

Can I make payments?

We accept some payment arrangements. Whether a payment arrangement is available depends on the bond and your situation. When part of the premium is paid after release, North Carolina law requires a written agreement, signed by both sides, that lists every payment date and amount, and you get a copy.

Does everyone qualify for a payment arrangement?

No. It depends on the size of the bond, the charge, and the cosigner's situation. Call us and we will tell you honestly what we can do for your bond.

What has to be in the written agreement?

North Carolina law (G.S. 58-71-167) requires the agreement to state the amount of premium still owed at release and the schedule: every payment date and the amount due on each date. It must be signed and dated by the defendant and the bondsman, and you are entitled to a copy.

What happens if a payment is missed?

Call us before the due date if you see a problem coming. Staying in touch matters. Under NC law, willfully failing to pay the agreed premium is one of the reasons a bondsman is allowed to surrender a defendant back to custody.

General information, not legal advice. Statutes referenced: N.C. Gen. Stat. §§ 58-71-167 and 58-71-20.
