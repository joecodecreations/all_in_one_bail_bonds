Domestic violence bail bonds in North Carolina

In a domestic violence case a judge sets the conditions of release, not a magistrate. That is why families are often told there is no bond yet.

Call (919) 247-9397Text us

The 48-hour rule

Under N.C. Gen. Stat. § 15A-534.1, a judge decides the release conditions in domestic violence cases. The person can be held for up to 48 hours after arrest until a judge does. If no judge has acted within 48 hours, a magistrate sets the conditions.

Conditions that come with release

The court can add conditions such as staying away from the other person or from a home, school or workplace. Breaking a condition can lead to a new arrest. Read the release order carefully and follow it exactly.

How we help

Call us as soon as you know about the arrest. We track when the bond is set and have the paperwork ready, so the bond can be posted as soon as there is one.

What it costs

North Carolina caps the bail bond premium at 15% of the bond amount. We accept some payment arrangements. See the cost calculator.

Questions

Why can't you post a bond right now?

Until a judge (or, after 48 hours, a magistrate) sets the conditions, there is no bond to post. We can get everything ready in the meantime.

Does the 48 hours mean they will be held for two full days?

Not necessarily. 48 hours is the maximum wait for a judge. If court is in session sooner, the conditions can be set sooner.

How much does a bail bond cost in North Carolina?

North Carolina law caps the bail bond premium at 15% of the bond amount (N.C. Gen. Stat. § 58-71-95). On a $5,000 bond the premium can be no more than $750. A bondsman may also ask for collateral, but may not charge extra application or processing fees. Call us for the exact cost of a specific bond.
