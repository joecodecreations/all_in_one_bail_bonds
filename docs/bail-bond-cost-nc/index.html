The 15% rule

In North Carolina the fee for a bail bond is called the premium. Under N.C. Gen. Stat. § 58-71-95 it "shall not exceed fifteen percent (15%) of the face amount of the bond." On a $5,000 bond, the premium can be no more than $750.

The same statute says a bondsman may not accept anything else of value from you except collateral. That means no application fees, travel fees or processing fees.

Cost questions

How much does a bail bond cost in North Carolina?

North Carolina law caps the bail bond premium at 15% of the bond amount (N.C. Gen. Stat. § 58-71-95). On a $5,000 bond the premium can be no more than $750. A bondsman may also ask for collateral, but may not charge extra application or processing fees. Call us for the exact cost of a specific bond.

Do I get the premium back when the case is over?

No. The premium is the fee for the bondsman's service and for taking on the risk of the full bond, so it is not returned when the case ends. Collateral is different: under NC law it must be returned within 15 days after the bondsman's liability on the bond finally ends.

Can I make payments?

We accept some payment arrangements. Whether a payment arrangement is available depends on the bond and your situation. When part of the premium is paid after release, North Carolina law requires a written agreement, signed by both sides, that lists every payment date and amount, and you get a copy.

Can a bondsman charge extra fees?

No. Under N.C. Gen. Stat. § 58-71-95 a bail bondsman may not accept anything of value except the premium, which is capped at 15%, and collateral. There are no lawful application, travel or processing fees on top.

What is collateral, and when do I get it back?

Collateral is property or money pledged to protect the bondsman if the defendant does not go to court. Not every bond needs it. The bondsman must give you a written receipt describing it and must return it within 15 days after liability on the bond finally ends.

If the judge lowers the bond later, do I get part of the premium back?

No. North Carolina law says the surety is not required to return any portion of the premium when a bond is reduced after it has been posted.

General information, not legal advice. Statutes referenced: N.C. Gen. Stat. §§ 58-71-95, 58-71-100, 58-71-16 and 58-71-167.
